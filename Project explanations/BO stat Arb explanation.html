One-Minute Overview

Cointegration-based statistical arbitrage profits from temporary mispricings in asset baskets tied to a long-run equilibrium. The calibration gap : static weights, disjoint parameter tuning, no adaptation to regime shifts . This is the central problem this paper solves via Gaussian Process Bayesian Optimisation.

Problem

The Calibration Gap

Johansen weights maximise a statistical criterion unrelated to trading profits. Execution parameters (entry thresholds, stop-loss, lookback) are set by heuristics. The cointegration relationship drifts , and static weights bleed when it does.

Approach

End-to-End GP-BO Pipeline

A Kalman filter tracks time-varying hedge ratios. Multi-tier entry-exit rules, volatility targeting, and regime detection are all calibrated jointly by a GP surrogate over $\theta=(e_1,e_2,e_3,z_\text{stop},L)$.

1 · The Calibration Problem

When a linear combination of non-stationary $I(1)$ prices is itself stationary $I(0)$, deviations from long-run equilibrium are temporary and tradeable. In practice, however, three compounding failures make this hard to exploit.

Failure 1

Static Weights

The Johansen eigenvector is estimated once on training data. Over 15 years, the 2020 oil crash, 2022 rate-hiking cycle, and sector rotations all shift true hedge ratios, static weights diverge silently.

Failure 2

Disjoint Calibration

Entry thresholds, stop-loss levels, and lookback windows are tuned separately, often by grid search, disconnected from the weight-estimation module. The objective of trading Sharpe which never happens to appear in the loop.

Failure 3

Black-Box Objective

Strategy performance is noisy, non-convex, gradient-free. Each evaluation costs a full backtest. Grid search in $d=5$ dimensions achieves only $O(T^{-1/d}) = T^{-0.2}$ convergence which is far too slow.

The Johansen VECM Foundation

The multivariate price dynamics are modelled as a Vector Error Correction Model:

$$\Delta y_t = \alpha\beta' y_{t-1} + \sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\Gamma_i \Delta y_{t-i} + \epsilon_t,\quad \epsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\Omega)$$

$\beta \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times r}$ holds the $r$ cointegrating vectors; $\alpha$ collects adjustment speeds. The trace test identifies rank $r$:

$$\text{LR}_\text{trace}(r) = -T\sum_{i=r+1}^{n}\ln(1-\hat\lambda_i)$$

For Precious Metals: $r=2$ at 95% confidence (trace statistics 70.37 and 37.87 vs. critical values 47.85 and 29.80). ADF test on the spread confirms stationarity with $p < 0.0001$ for all five universes.

Mean-reversion half-life : informs the lookback window $L$ and maximum holding period $2h$:

$$h = -\frac{\ln 2}{\ln(1+\hat\phi)}$$

$\hat\phi < 0$ from the ADF regression ensures $h > 0$. Lookback $L \approx \text{small multiple of } h$.

Multi-Tier Entry-Exit Rules

The rolling z-score of the Kalman spread is normalised as $z_t = (S_t - \bar S_{t,L})/\hat\sigma_{t,L}$. Rather than binary entry at a single threshold, a three-tier scheme allocates position size gradually:

$$p_t^\text{long} = \begin{cases}0 & z_t > -e_1 \\ \tfrac{1}{3} & -e_2 < z_t \le -e_1 \\ \tfrac{2}{3} & -e_3 < z_t \le -e_2 \\ 1 & z_t \le -e_3\end{cases}\qquad p_t^\text{short} = -p_t^\text{long}\text{ (mirrored)}$$

Combined position $p_t = p_t^\text{long}+p_t^\text{short} \in [-1,+1]$. Stop-loss exits when $|z_t|>z_\text{stop}$ or holding period exceeds $2h$.

Volatility targeting overlay (Barroso & Santa-Clara, 2015):

$$r_t^{VT} = r_t^\text{net} \times \min\!\left(\frac{\sigma^*}{\hat\sigma_t^\text{lag}},\, 3.0\right),\quad \hat\sigma_t^\text{lag} = \text{RollingStd}(r^\text{net},63)_{t-1}\times\sqrt{252}$$

Target $\sigma^*=15\%$, leverage capped at $3\times$, 63-day lagged volatility to prevent look-ahead.

Transaction cost model (proportional, 15 bp one-way):

$$r_t^\text{net} = r_t^\text{gross} - c\cdot|\Delta p_t|,\quad c=15\text{ bp},\quad c^* = \frac{\text{Gross PnL}}{\sum_t|\Delta p_t|}$$

3 · Adaptive Hedge Ratios via Kalman Filter

The Kalman filter is the single most consequential component. Removing it collapses the Precious Metals Sharpe signal from 5.871 to −0.301. It treats hedge ratios as hidden states that evolve stochastically, tracking market-regime drift without retraining.

State-Space Formulation

Designate one asset $y_t$ as dependent and collect remaining $n-1$ log-prices into $x_t$:

Transition equation : ratios follow a random walk:

$$\beta_t = \beta_{t-1} + w_t,\quad w_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\delta I_{n-1}),\quad \delta=10^{-4}$$

Observation equation:

$$y_t = x_t'\beta_t + v_t,\quad v_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, V_e),\quad V_e=10^{-3}$$

Recursive Update (Predict → Gain → Update)

Prediction: $\hat\beta_{t|t-1} = \hat\beta_{t-1|t-1}$, $\quad P_{t|t-1} = P_{t-1|t-1} + \delta I_{n-1}$

Kalman gain:

$$K_t = \frac{P_{t|t-1}x_t}{x_t'P_{t|t-1}x_t + V_e} \in \mathbb{R}^{n-1}$$

Update: $\hat\beta_{t|t} = \hat\beta_{t|t-1} + K_t(y_t - x_t'\hat\beta_{t|t-1})$, $\quad P_{t|t} = (I - K_t x_t')P_{t|t-1}$

Spread: $S_t = y_t - x_t'\hat\beta_{t|t}$ , the Kalman regression residual, mean-reverting under valid cointegration.

The filter is seeded on training data. Its terminal state $(\hat\beta_{T_\text{train}}, P_{T_\text{train}})$ is carried forward unchanged into the OOS period , a strict information barrier.

4 · Gaussian Process Bayesian Optimisation

GP-BO maintains a surrogate of the full backtest objective, exploring the 5-dimensional parameter space far more efficiently than grid search. It converges within ~10 evaluations to near-optimal parameters.

Objective Function & GP Surrogate

Objective : penalised Sharpe on training data:

$$f(\theta) = \underbrace{\frac{\bar r(\theta)}{\hat\sigma_r(\theta)}\sqrt{252}}_{\text{Annualised Sharpe}} - \underbrace{\lambda \cdot p_\text{ADF}(\theta)}_{\text{Stationarity penalty}},\quad \lambda=1.0$$

$p_\text{ADF}(\theta)$ is the ADF $p$-value for the in-sample spread. The penalty rarely activates: $p_\text{ADF}<0.001$ in $>95\%$ of evaluations.

GP posterior : Matérn 5/2 kernel, observation noise $\sigma_n^2=0.01$:

$$\mu_t(\theta) = k_t(\theta)^\top(K_t+\sigma_n^2 I)^{-1}y_t,\qquad \sigma_t^2(\theta) = k(\theta,\theta)-k_t(\theta)^\top(K_t+\sigma_n^2 I)^{-1}k_t(\theta)$$

Expected Improvement acquisition:

$$\text{EI}(\theta) = (\mu_t(\theta)-f^+)\Phi(Z) + \sigma_t(\theta)\phi(Z),\quad Z=\frac{\mu_t(\theta)-f^+}{\sigma_t(\theta)}$$

$f^+ = \max_{i\le t}y_i$. The next query: $\theta_{t+1} = \arg\max_{\theta\in\mathcal X}\text{EI}(\theta)$.

Convergence Guarantee vs. Grid Search

GP-BO simple regret (Srinivas et al., 2010):

$$\epsilon_T = \min_{t\le T}[f(\theta^*)-f(\theta_t)] = \mathcal O\!\left(\frac{(\log T)^{(d+1)/2}}{\sqrt{T}}\right) \quad \text{(polylogarithmic)}$$

Grid search over $[0,1]^d$: only $\mathcal O(T^{-1/d})$ for $d=5$ this is $T^{-0.2}$, far slower.

At 20 evaluations (⅓ budget), BO reaches Sharpe 5.62; random search only hits this at evaluation ~45.

7 · Discussion & Limitations

Interpretation

Signal-Normalised Sharpe

Returns are measured per unit of z-score movement, not per dollar invested. For a fully funded implementation, capital-return Sharpe likely falls in the 1.5–3.0 range depending on leverage and notional sizing. The numbers measure signal quality, not portfolio return.

Boundary Convergence

22/25 Parameters at Bounds

$e_1$ saturates at 0.80σ in every universe. $e_2$ and $e_3$ hit their ceilings in four of five cases. BO converges to early single-tier entry with rare scaling , not diverse universe-specific profiles. The current search space may be too narrow, or the objective landscape flat along threshold dimensions.

Known Issue

Short-Side Sign Error

A sign-convention error in the middle tier of short partial-exit logic assigns +⅓ instead of −⅓. Affects ~8–12% of short trade observations. Creates modest upward bias in Sharpe signals, estimated at 3–8%. Qualitative conclusions (Kalman dominance, baseline underperformance) are unlikely to reverse.

Evidence Against Overfitting

Five independent lines of evidence argue against overfitting:

  1. Cross-universe consistency : five independently optimised universes, all positive OOS Sharpe
  2. Walk-forward stability : within 4% of single-shot estimates over 23 windows
  3. Sub-period consistency : 20/20 cells positive across all universes and macro regimes
  4. Harvey haircut : minimum |t| = 8.46, vs. required 3.0
  5. Break-even cost : 470 bp for Precious Metals, 30× the assumed 15 bp

The Energy Paradox

The Energy universe has Johansen rank 0 at 95% confidence which is formally no cointegrating vector and yet produces the second-highest OOS Sharpe (6.339). The Kalman filter does not require a formal cointegration finding: it tracks episodic co-movement induced by crude-price shocks regardless. The framework is more general than the "cointegration" label implies, an adaptive relative-value strategy useful even when formal cointegration cannot be established.

Suggested Extensions

Surrogate

Deep Kernel Learning

Replace the GP surrogate with Bayesian neural networks or deep kernel learning (Wilson et al., 2016) to capture non-stationarities in the objective landscape beyond Matérn smoothness.

Multi-Objective

Expected Hypervolume Improvement

Multi-objective BO (Daulton et al., 2020) to jointly optimise Sharpe, drawdown, and turnover, surfacing the full Pareto frontier rather than a single Sharpe-maximising parameter set.

Coverage

Full Walk-Forward + Fixed Baselines

Extend walk-forward to all five universes. Re-implement EG and OU-MLE baselines under the identical signal-normalised return convention. Correct the short-side sign error and re-run.
